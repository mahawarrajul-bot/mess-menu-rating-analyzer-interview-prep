  How the statistics work

  1. Bayesian average (stats/bayes.py)
  - Idea: Every dish starts with 10 imaginary ratings equal to the overall
    average. The real ratings are added to those.
  - Formula: score = (10·avg + sum of real ratings) / (10 + n).
  - Effect: With few ratings the score stays near the overall average. With many
    it approaches the dish's own average.
  - Example from the run: Paneer Special has two 5★ ratings, so its raw average
    is 5.00, rank 1. Its Bayesian score is 3.81, rank 6, with a wide interval of
    3.28–4.35.
  - Interval: It narrows as ratings accumulate, because it uses σ/√(10+n).

  2. Trend (stats/trend.py)
  - Method: Take each serving day's average rating and fit a straight line over
    time. A t-test on the slope gives a p-value.
  - Why daily averages: Ratings from one meal come from the same batch of food,
    so they aren't independent. Using them individually would fake significance.
  - Many dishes at once: Some would look significant by luck, so I apply a
    Benjamini–Hochberg correction across dishes.
  - When a trend is reported: Only with at least 30 ratings, 6 serving days and
    a 14-day span. The adjusted p must be under 0.05, and the change must be at
    least 0.3 stars per 28 days.
  - Example from the run: Dal Fry is flagged "declining" at −0.94 stars per 28
    days.

  3. Weekday pattern (stats/weekday.py)
  - Method: Subtract each dish's own score from each rating, so a weekday isn't
    blamed for the dishes served on it. Then run a one-way ANOVA across
    weekdays.
  - Example from the run: Wednesday is found with F = 26.2 and p = 5.5×10⁻³¹.
    The effect is small, though: η² is only 2%.
  - Interval rule: The weakest day is named only if its interval is entirely
    below zero.

  4. Recommendations (stats/recommend.py)
  - Order: Need-more-data, then Drop, then Keep, then Watch.
  - Thresholds: All of them are in config/stats.json.

  5. Simulator (simulator/)
  - Setup: 120 students, 4 Mon–Sun weeks, lunch and dinner. Each rating is the
    dish's true quality plus a weekday effect, a student bias and noise.
  - Planted patterns:
    - Paneer Special has two 5★ ratings.
    - Dal Fry declines from 4.2 to 3.1.
    - Wednesday gets −0.45.
    - Veg Biryani starts in week 3.
    - Rajma Masala and Aloo Paratha are good, and Lauki Sabzi is poor with
      oily/bland tags.

  Limitations to know before the viva

  - Weekday size is understated. The weekday effect only shows up in dishes
    served on several weekdays, such as Roti and Rice. Wednesday comes out at
    −0.27 against the planted −0.45. Detection works, but the size is too small.
  - I changed the simulated menu to make Dal Fry's decline detectable. With Dal
    Fry served on Wednesdays, the Wednesday dip added noise and 3 of the first
    12 test seeds missed the decline. I moved it to Mon/Thu/Sat instead of
    changing the spec'd method. The better fix would be to adjust daily means
    for weekday before the trend test. That changes specs/design.md §5.2, so I
    haven't done it. Do you want it?
  - Normal approximation. The Bayesian average treats 1–5 ratings as normally
    distributed with the same σ for every dish, and C = 10 is a choice, not a
    law. The Dirichlet–multinomial model is the more exact alternative.
